परीक्षाparīkṣā: examination

What happens at your first visit, from the door to the plan in your hand

A first consultation takes about 45 minutes. No treatment starts on that day without being explained to you first. Here is the whole visit, in order.

  1. Before you come

    Book, and gather what you have

    Book by phone or WhatsApp, or use the booking form below. Bring old reports and scans, any prescriptions, and your current medicines (the strips or bottles are fine). Eat as you normally do; you do not need to fast for a first consultation.

  2. First 5 minutes

    Arrival at the desk

    We note your name, age and phone number and make a paper case card. If you came with a parent or child, they can sit in with you.

  3. About 15 minutes

    The conversation

    The vaidya asks about the complaint that brought you, how long you have had it, and what makes it better or worse. Then come questions about sleep, appetite, digestion, bowel habits, energy through the day, what you eat and how you work. They ask about every medicine you take.

  4. About 10 minutes

    The examination

    नाडीपरीक्षा Nadi pariksha means reading the pulse at the wrist. The vaidya also looks at your tongue, skin and eyes, and reads your reports. From all of this they judge your प्रकृती prakriti, your natural constitution, which Ayurveda uses to choose food, routine and treatment for you.

  5. About 10 minutes

    What they found, in plain words

    The vaidya tells you what they understood from the history and examination, and whether Ayurvedic care looks suitable. If your problem needs a blood test, a scan or a specialist first, they say so and send you there. We would rather lose a patient than delay one.

  6. Last 5 to 10 minutes

    Your plan, written down

    You leave with a written plan: medicines if they are needed, changes to food, a daily routine, what to avoid, and the date to come back. If a therapy or Panchakarma is advised, you hear the number of days and the full cost before you agree. Fees are paid at the desk, as listed under Fees.

Alongside your current doctor

We work alongside your doctor, and do not replace them. Do not stop or change any medicine your doctor has prescribed without talking to them first. Tell us everything you take. If something we plan could interact with it, we will say so, and with your permission we will write to your doctor.

चिकित्साcikitsā: treatment

The treatments, explained without jargon

Below is what each treatment involves, how long it takes and how it feels. The vaidya decides after examining you whether a therapy is suitable for you.

Not every therapy is suitable for everyone, including during pregnancy, for frail older people and children, or for people with heart, kidney or bleeding conditions. The vaidya may advise against a therapy.

Panchakarma

पंचकर्म Panchakarma means the five cleansing procedures. It is done only after a consultation, under a vaidya's supervision. Tap a name to read what it involves.

Vamana वमन Programme of about 7 to 11 days

Medicated, supervised vomiting, done once, after several days of oil intake and massage that prepare the body. It is an intensive procedure. You rest afterwards and eat a very simple diet for the next few days.

Time: 3 to 5 days of preparation, one procedure day, then 3 to 5 days of a simple, gradually widening diet.

Virechana विरेचन Programme of about 7 to 11 days

A controlled, medicated purge, again after days of oil intake and massage. You are watched throughout the procedure day, and you rest afterwards.

Time: 3 to 5 days of preparation, one procedure day, then 3 to 5 days of graded diet.

Basti बस्ति Course of 8 to 15 days

A medicated oil or herbal decoction given as an enema. You lie on your side on the therapy table. Each sitting takes about 30 minutes, followed by a short rest.

Time: one sitting a day for 8 to 15 days, with food guidance throughout.

Nasya नस्य Course of 7 to 14 days

Drops of medicated oil or powder into the nose, after a gentle face and neck massage and a short steam. You lie back with your head tilted. You may feel warmth, and sometimes a mild tingling.

Time: about 30 minutes a day, for 7 to 14 days.

Raktamokshana रक्तमोक्षण One sitting, repeated only if the vaidya advises

A small, controlled amount of blood is let out at a chosen site by the physician. It is used selectively. Only with single-use sterile equipment; not advised in pregnancy or anaemia. If you take blood thinners or have a bleeding problem, tell us at the first visit.

Time: 30 to 45 minutes per sitting.

A full Panchakarma programme usually runs 7 to 21 days. The vaidya sets the length after your consultation and quotes the cost before you decide.

Supportive therapies

These are done on their own or as preparation for Panchakarma, by appointment only.

Abhyanga अभ्यंग45 to 60 minutes
A full-body massage with warm herbal oil, chosen by the vaidya to suit your constitution. You lie on a wooden table. The pressure is firm but gentle.
Swedana स्वेदन15 to 20 minutes
Herbal steam or sweating, usually right after Abhyanga and booked together with it.
Shirodhara शिरोधारा30 to 45 minutes
A thin, steady stream of warm oil or medicated liquid is poured over the forehead while you lie down with your eyes covered. It is quiet and slow.
Kati basti कटिबस्ति30 to 40 minutes
Warm oil is held on the lower back inside a ring of dough while you lie face down.
Janu basti जानुबस्ति30 to 40 minutes
The same method, done at the knee.
Patra pinda sweda पत्रपिंड स्वेद45 to 60 minutes
Warm bundles of herbs and leaves, heated in oil, are pressed over the body.

Diet and daily routine

Much of what a vaidya advises is about how you live. दिनचर्या Dinacharya is the daily routine: when to wake, eat, work and sleep. ऋतुचर्या Ritucharya is the seasonal routine: how food and habits shift with the monsoon, winter and summer. You get both in writing at your consultation, fitted to your own day.

प्रश्नोत्तरpraśnottara: questions and answers

Questions patients and their families ask

Do I need to stop my regular medicines?

No. Do not stop or change any prescribed medicine without talking to the doctor who prescribed it. This matters most for medicines for blood pressure, diabetes, the heart, thyroid and epilepsy. Bring them to the first visit, and the vaidya will plan around them.

Is Panchakarma painful?

Most of it is not. Massage, steam and oil treatments are usually relaxing. Vamana and Virechana can be uncomfortable on the day, and Basti can feel strange the first time. A vaidya or trained therapist is with you throughout, and you can ask to pause at any point.

What should I eat before a therapy?

On massage, steam and Shirodhara days, have a light, warm, home-cooked meal about 2 to 3 hours before. Do not come on a completely empty stomach, or after a heavy or fried meal. Panchakarma days have their own food instructions, which the vaidya gives you. Tell us if you have diabetes.

Do you have women therapists?

Yes. Women patients are treated by women therapists, and you may ask for Vd. Meera Kulkarni at consultation. Tell us when you book.

Can I bring my parent, or come on their behalf?

Yes, you can bring them, and a family member can sit in on the consultation. If your parent finds stairs difficult, say so when you book and we will plan the visit around it.

How soon will I feel better?

We cannot promise a result or a timeline. People respond differently, and Ayurvedic care is usually gradual. At your consultation the vaidya tells you what to watch for, and when to come back for a review.

Are your medicines made at the clinic?

Medicines are prepared on the premises according to classical texts. The vaidya tells you what each medicine is and how to take it, and you may ask to see the ingredients.

Do I need an appointment?

Therapies are by appointment only. For a consultation, booking saves you waiting. If you walk in during clinic hours, we will see you as soon as there is a gap.

Which languages do you speak?

Marathi, Hindi and English. Your written plan is given in the language you prefer.
