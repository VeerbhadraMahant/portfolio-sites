Same steel plates since 1998.

Who started this place?

Sanjay Kale, a state-level powerlifter, who wanted a room where lifters could simply train. His son Rohit runs the floor with him now.

The name is a nod to the akhada, the mud-floor wrestling ring where Sanjay grew up watching dawn practice in a Kothrud talim before he ever picked up a barbell.

  1. 1998

    One rack, one godown

    Sanjay rents a ground-floor godown off a Kothrud lane. A local fabricator welds the first rack from pipe. The plates come second-hand from a Bhosari scrapyard. Eleven members, all from the neighbourhood.

  2. 2003

    The hall we're still in

    The club moves to its current hall. The first deadlift platform is plywood and rubber matting from a truck-tyre dealer. It has been rebuilt twice. It's still the platform people queue for.

  3. 2009

    A clerk pulls 100 kg

    A 52-year-old bank clerk, who joined to fix his back, pulls his first 100 kg deadlift. The whole floor stops to watch. Sanjay still tells this one.

  4. 2014

    The women's batch

    Three members' mothers ask if they can train too, just not at 7 pm with a full floor. Monday, Wednesday, Friday, 11:30 am. It never stopped.

  5. 2020

    Five months shut

    Lockdown closes the gym. Sanjay doesn't charge for the closed months and oils the plates every week. When the shutters go up, the same people walk in.

  6. 2022

    Rohit takes the morning batch

    Rohit comes back from a sports-science course and takes over the early shift. He adds the sled lane, the air bikes and the rule that nobody trains hurt without telling a coach.

  7. Today

    Four racks, same plates

    Four power racks, two platforms and a sled lane. The 20 kg steel plates from 1998 are still in rotation, a bit smoother where thousands of hands have been.

"Keep it simple. You lift, we watch, you come back."

Sanjay Kale, founder

What happens on day one?

A first visit takes about 75 minutes, costs nothing, and ends whenever you want it to. Nobody sells you anything.

  1. 1

    Message us

    Send "free first session" on WhatsApp with your name and a time. We confirm the slot the same day.

  2. 2

    Ten-minute chat at the desk

    Rohit or Sanjay asks what you want, what hurts, and how much time you really have. Chai is available if it's cold.

  3. 3

    Warm up with a coach

    Fifteen minutes of mobility and light movement. The coach watches how you move, and decides what to teach first.

  4. 4

    Three lifts, light

    Usually a squat pattern, a hinge and a push or pull. Light weight, slow reps. The point is the pattern, not the numbers.

  5. 5

    Go home and decide

    Join at the desk if you want to, or go home and think it over. Thank you for coming either way.

Things people ask before they come

Not here? WhatsApp us. A real person answers, usually Rohit.

I've never lifted. Will I be the only beginner?

No. Roughly a third of the people here started in the last six months. Coaches run beginner sessions on the floor, so you'll see other first-timers.

Is there a contract or lock-in?

Every plan is paid up front for the period you pick. When it ends you choose whether to renew, and nothing renews on its own.

Are there any other fees?

Only the one-time admission of ₹500. Chalk, lockers and coaching on the floor are included. Personal training is the ₹6,000 plan.

Is there a women-only time?

Yes. Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 11:30 am to 1:00 pm, with a woman coach. Women also train in the regular hours.

I have a bad back or knee. Can I still train?

Usually yes. Tell the coach on day one and bring any doctor's or physio's note. We start light and work around it.

Is it hot in summer? Is there AC?

There is no AC, only big fans and open shutters. Come early morning or after 7 pm in May. People do.

Can I pay by UPI? Can I pause my plan?

UPI and cash both work. If you're away for a month or more, tell us and we'll talk. We'd rather extend your plan than argue.

Where do I park?

Two-wheelers outside the gate. Cars on the lane, if there's space. Most people come by bike.
